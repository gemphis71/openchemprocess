# WRKUP-001｜Workup Control Authority

## Control Authority and Engineering Criteria for Quench Reactions in the Workup Stage

---

## 1. Positioning

In most processes, workup is used only to process the results of a completed reaction.  
Workup is upgraded to a **source of control authority** only when all of the following conditions are simultaneously met:

- The primary reaction has nominally stopped;
- Reactive species still exist in the Reaction End State;
- The Chemically Stable Final State is not directly generated by the primary reaction;
- The Chemically Stable Final State can only be obtained through a quench reaction.

In such systems:

> **Whether the reaction has truly ended is determined by whether the quench reaction has been completed, not by whether the primary reaction has stopped.**

---

## 2. Foundational Criteria

### Criterion 2.1｜Reaction End State Criterion

If any of the following states exist in the system at the end of the reaction:

- Reactive intermediates;
- Metallic or organometallic complex states;
- Reversible catalytic systems;
- Reaction end state species awaiting transformation;

then this state does not constitute a Chemically Stable Final State.

---

### Criterion 2.2｜Quench Reaction Criterion

If the system meets any of the following conditions:

- The quench process has a non-negligible kinetic time scale;
- The quench pathway is not unique and involves competing reactions;
- The quench depends on temperature, pH, or phase conditions;

then the quench must be treated as a **chemical reaction that requires explicit design and validation**.

---

## 3. Three Failure Modes of Quench Control in WRKUP

### Type I｜Chemically Incomplete Quench  
**Chemically Incomplete Quench**

**Definition**  
A quench agent has been added, but under the given time, temperature, or conditions, the quench reaction is not complete and the system retains chemical activity.

**Necessary Conditions**

- The quench is a chemical reaction rather than an instantaneous operation;
- The reaction rate is correlated with scale;
- The failure mode is easily overlooked at lab scale.

**Machine-Oriented Decision Points**

- “Quench agent added” cannot be used as the termination criterion;
- The completion of the quench reaction must be evaluated under the design conditions.

---

### Type II｜Loss of Control in the Critical Time Window  
**Partial Quench / Pathway Competition**

**Definition**  
A reactive window exists in the early stage of quenching. Under scale-up conditions, this window is elongated, causing a shift in the dominance of reaction pathways.

**Key Features**

- Early-stage quench conditions ≠ late-stage quench conditions;
- Scale-up causes a significant increase in addition time;
- Side reactions are amplified in the early quench stage.

**Machine-Oriented Decision Points**

- Evaluate whether a reactive window exists from t = 0 until complete deactivation;
- Do not treat the final state (e.g., final pH) as the sole criterion.

---

### Type III｜Apparent Quench due to Physical Inaccessibility  
**Physically Inaccessible Quench**

**Definition**  
The quench reagent is nominally added to the system, but due to phase change or interfacial issues, it does not actually enter the reaction phase to participate in the quench reaction.

**Notes**

- Physical causes (freezing, phase separation);
- Chemical consequence (incomplete quench);
- Amplified under scale-up conditions.

---

## 4. Representative Cases (Unchanged, Language Calibrated Only)

### Case 1｜Slow Chemical Quench in DCC Coupling (Type I)

**Decision Points**

- Hydrolysis of DCC-derived active intermediates is a slow reaction;
- Kinetics are insufficient under neutral conditions;
- “Addition of water” is not a criterion for quench completion.

**Control Conclusion**

- Acidic conditions and sufficient residence time must be provided;
- Phase separation is allowed only after the active intermediates are fully deactivated.

`#Audit: The criterion for quench completion is the disappearance of active intermediates, not the addition of the quench agent.`

---

### Case 2｜Physical Removal ≠ Chemical Quench in Enzymatic Systems (Type I)

**Decision Points**

- Filtration is only a physical operation;
- Residual soluble enzyme can retain catalytic activity.

**Control Conclusion**

- A chemical or thermal inactivation step must be introduced;
- Otherwise, the reaction logic continues to exist through the workup stage.

---

### Case 3｜Loss of Control in the Critical Window Caused by Gradual Acid Addition to a Basic System (Type II)

**Decision Points**

- During the early stage of quenching, the bulk system remains basic;
- Scale-up significantly elongates this window;
- Base-sensitive species undergo side reactions.

**Control Conclusion**

- A reactive window must not exist at any time point;
- Recommended reverse quench: reaction mixture → excess acidic system.

`#Audit: The criterion for quench control is not the final pH, but the absence of a reactive window from t = 0.`

---

### Case 4｜Quench Completion Issues in Metallic/Organometallic Systems (Type I)

**Decision Points**

- The Reaction End State is a metallic complex intermediate;
- Stable products are generated only after quenching.

**Control Conclusion**

- The metal deactivation pathway must be explicitly designed;
- No subsequent operation is allowed before deactivation is complete.

---

### Case 5｜Physically Inaccessible Quench under Low-Temperature Conditions (Type III)

**Decision Points**

- Under scale-up conditions, the quench reagent may freeze or become interfacially isolated;
- The reagent is apparently added but does not participate in the reaction.

**Control Conclusion**

- Physical accessibility under scale-up conditions must be validated at lab scale;
- Otherwise, the quench strategy is not scalable.

`#Audit: Apparent addition is not actual participation; physical accessibility is a prerequisite for quench completion.`

---

## 5. Blocking Rules

### Rule W-1｜Slow Chemical Quench Block

If the quench reaction cannot be completed under the design conditions, proceeding to subsequent steps is prohibited.

### Rule W-2｜Critical Window Block

If a reactive window exists during quenching and is elongated during scale-up, the protocol is unacceptable.

### Rule W-3｜Physical Accessibility Block

If the quench reagent poses a risk of freezing, phase separation, or interfacial isolation under scale-up conditions, scale-up is prohibited.

---

## 6. Methodology Summary (Machine-Oriented)

> **Whether a reaction has truly ended is a determinable engineering problem.  
> The basis for determination is not whether the operation is finished, but whether the quench reaction is complete.**

The goal of WRKUP-001 is to provide machines with the capability to:

- Identify unscalable quenching strategies during the design phase;
- Correctly backtrack to the workup layer when anomalies occur;
- Eliminate scale-up non-reproducibility from the design root.

---

## #Audit

This document is used to determine whether workup constitutes a reaction-termination control layer.  
Core criterion:

**Whether the quench is designed and validated as a completable chemical reaction.**
